Aggregating results with GROUP BY

So far every query has returned rows. Analysts usually want answers: total revenue, orders per region, customers per month. That's what aggregation does. You already met COUNT(*) — now add SUM(), AVG(), and their sidekick GROUP BY.

Quick check before you practice:

Which clause decides the "buckets" that aggregates fall into?

Try it on real data

Your turn: revenue per region, highest first. The bench below is pre-loaded with the ecommerce dataset — check the schema panel if you forget a column name.

SELECT region, COUNT(*) AS orders, ROUND(SUM(total_amount), 2) AS revenue  FROM orders GROUP BY region;

Stuck? Reveal one approach 
SELECT c.region,
       COUNT(*) AS orders,
       ROUND(SUM(o.total_amount), 2) AS revenue
FROM orders o
JOIN customers c ON c.id = o.customer_id
GROUP BY c.region
ORDER BY revenue DESC;

Notice the drill above doesn't tell you right or wrong — it shows what came back, and the "what you should see" table is your mirror. When they match, move on.
